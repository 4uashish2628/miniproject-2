Make each recording useful

  1. Keep the entire body in view, with good lighting and a fixed camera.
  2. Use front or slight diagonal views. Include a diagonal view for seated poses and squats.
  3. Record one activity per clip, at a comfortable pace. Begin moving before recording.
  4. Repeat across two sessions. Keep the participant ID unchanged.
  5. Reserve validation and test participants before collecting your final dataset.

Understand your project.

A small, explainable pipeline with a testable research question.

The question

Do temporal pose features improve recognition of six activities compared with mean pose features, when the test participant is unseen?

The pipeline

Camera or video→33 landmarks→18 pose features→2-second window→Random Forest

What we build

Aspect-corrected pose geometry, temporal statistics, a labeled collection tool, participant-separated evaluation, visibility rejection, label confirmation, and repetition counting. The pose detector is Google's pretrained MediaPipe model.

Six activities

Standing, sitting, marching in place, squats, jumping jacks, and one-hand waving. This is a controlled single-person study. It does not recognize every everyday action.

What to explain in a viva

Pose estimation locates body joints. Activity recognition assigns a label to motion over time. The pretrained detector solves the first task. Our features and trained classifier solve the second.

Research foundations

Bazarevsky et al. · BlazePose (2020)
Arrowsmith et al. · Physiotherapy Exercise Classification (2023)

Limitations

Single camera and person, projected angles, occlusion, camera-view sensitivity, small local dataset, and a two-second recognition delay. Low confidence rejection cannot guarantee recognition of unseen activity types. Repetition counts are demonstrative, not medical assessments.
